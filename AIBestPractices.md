# AI Best Practices

Four principles for using AI tools productively and safely at work. They apply to every tool and every task.

| Principle | In one line |
| --- | --- |
| Security | Protect confidential and personal information |
| Accuracy | Check the output before you rely on it |
| Transparency | Be open about where AI has been used |
| Continuous Learning | Keep experimenting and share what works |

---

## Security

Do not share confidential or sensitive information with AI systems unless authorised.

**Do**

- Use only the AI tools your organisation has approved
- Check what your organisation allows before entering work information
- Remove names and identifying details when the task does not need them
- Treat AI tools like any other external service: if you would not email it outside the company, do not paste it in

**Do not**

- Enter passwords, access keys or security details
- Enter personal data about customers, colleagues or members of the public unless the tool is approved for it
- Enter financial, legal or commercially sensitive material unless the tool is approved for it
- Use a personal account for work information

If you think sensitive information has been entered into a tool that is not approved for it, report it to your service desk or security team straight away. Reporting quickly matters more than how it happened.

## Accuracy

Always validate outputs before making business decisions.

AI tools can produce answers that sound confident and are wrong. They can invent facts, figures, quotations and references, and their knowledge may be out of date.

**Do**

- Check facts, figures, dates and names against a trusted source
- Ask the tool to show its reasoning or sources, then check them
- Test any formula, script or code before using it
- Read the whole output before you send or publish it
- Take extra care with legal, financial, medical and safety topics

**Remember**

- You are responsible for the work you send, however it was produced
- The more important the decision, the more thorough the check should be

## Transparency

Clearly identify when AI-generated content has been used.

**Do**

- Follow your organisation's policy on when and how to disclose the use of AI
- Tell colleagues when a draft or analysis was produced with AI, so that they know how carefully to review it
- Keep a note of how AI was used for important documents and decisions
- Be honest if asked whether AI was involved

**Do not**

- Present AI output as checked or verified when it has not been
- Use AI to make a decision about a person without human review

## Continuous Learning

Experiment regularly and learn from successful implementations.

**Do**

- Start with low-risk tasks and build up
- Try the same task with different prompts and compare the results. See [Prompt Engineering Basics](PromptEngineeringBasics.md)
- Save prompts that work well and share them with your team
- Share what did not work too, so that others can avoid it
- Revisit tasks from time to time, because tools improve quickly

---

## Before You Use an AI Output

- [ ] Is the tool approved for this kind of information?
- [ ] Have I left out anything confidential that the task did not need?
- [ ] Have I checked the facts and figures?
- [ ] Have I read the whole output, not just the first paragraph?
- [ ] Does it sound like me or my organisation, and is the tone right?
- [ ] Do I need to say that AI was used?
- [ ] Would I be comfortable explaining how this was produced?

## When Not to Use AI

- When the information is too sensitive for the tools you have been given
- When the task needs a decision about a person, such as hiring, discipline or performance, without human review
- When you cannot check the answer and a mistake would matter
- When your organisation's policy says not to
