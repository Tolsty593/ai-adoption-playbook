# Adoption Framework

A structured approach to introducing AI into teams and organisations.

Giving people access to an AI tool is the easy part. Adoption succeeds when people know what the tool is for, trust that they are allowed to use it, and can get help when they are stuck. This framework treats AI adoption as a service to be introduced and supported, not as a one-off installation.

The framework has six phases. Each one has a goal, a set of activities and a clear output that the next phase depends on.

| Phase | Goal | Main output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Assess | Understand needs, readiness and risks | A shortlist of use cases |
| 2. Govern | Agree the rules before anyone starts | An acceptable use guide |
| 3. Pilot | Prove value with a small group | Pilot results and a decision |
| 4. Enable | Give people the skills and confidence | Training and a champions network |
| 5. Roll out | Extend access in stages, with support ready | A supported live service |
| 6. Measure and improve | Check that it is used and useful | Regular reviews and updates |

---

## Phase 1: Assess

**Goal:** understand where AI can help, and whether the organisation is ready.

**Activities**

- Talk to teams about the tasks that take the most time or cause the most frustration
- List possible use cases and score each one for value, effort and risk
- Review what data each use case would involve, and how sensitive it is
- Check what tools people are already using, including unofficial ones
- Identify a sponsor with the authority to make decisions

**Questions to answer**

- What problem are we trying to solve?
- Who benefits, and how will we know?
- What data is involved, and are we allowed to use it this way?
- What happens if the AI output is wrong?

**Output:** a shortlist of two or three use cases with clear value and low risk.

## Phase 2: Govern

**Goal:** agree the rules before anyone starts, so that people can use AI with confidence.

**Activities**

- Confirm which tools are approved and who may use them
- Define what information must never be entered into an AI tool
- State who is accountable for checking AI output before it is used
- Agree when the use of AI should be disclosed
- Involve security, data protection and legal colleagues early
- Decide how the rules will be reviewed as tools change

**Output:** a short acceptable use guide, written in plain language, that fits on one or two pages. See [AI Best Practices](AIBestPractices.md) for the core principles.

## Phase 3: Pilot

**Goal:** prove the value with a small group before investing in a full rollout.

**Activities**

- Choose a pilot group with a mix of confident and cautious users
- Agree what success looks like before the pilot starts
- Run the pilot for a fixed period, with a named contact for questions
- Collect feedback every week: what worked, what did not, what was confusing
- Record the questions people ask, because everyone else will ask them too

**Output:** pilot results measured against the success criteria, and a decision to extend, adjust or stop.

## Phase 4: Enable

**Goal:** give people the skills and confidence to use AI well.

**Activities**

- Provide a short introduction for all users. See [Getting Started with AI](GettingStarted.md)
- Teach practical prompt writing using real examples from each team. See [Prompt Engineering Basics](PromptEngineeringBasics.md)
- Recruit champions: enthusiastic users in each team who share tips and collect feedback
- Build a library of prompts and examples that have worked
- Offer training in more than one format, such as live sessions, short guides and drop-in clinics

**Output:** training materials, a champions network, and a shared library of examples.

## Phase 5: Roll out

**Goal:** extend access in stages, with support ready before users need it.

**Activities**

- Roll out team by team rather than to everyone at once
- Brief the service desk before each stage, and give them answers to the common questions. See [Common User Questions](CommonUserQuestions.md)
- Add AI tools to the service catalogue, with a clear route for requests and problems
- Tell users what the tool is for, what it is not for, and where to get help
- Keep a way for users to report incorrect or worrying output

**Output:** a live service that users can access, understand and get support for.

## Phase 6: Measure and improve

**Goal:** check that AI is being used and is useful, and keep improving.

| What to measure | How | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adoption | Active users as a share of licensed users | Whether people are using it at all |
| Depth of use | How often people use it, and for which tasks | Whether it has become part of real work |
| Value | Time saved on agreed tasks, reported by users and managers | Whether it is worth the cost |
| Confidence | Short surveys before and after training | Whether enablement is working |
| Support demand | Number and type of AI-related tickets | Where guidance is missing or unclear |
| Risk | Reported incidents and breaches of the usage guide | Whether governance is working |

**Activities**

- Review the measures on a regular schedule and share the results
- Update training and guidance based on the questions the service desk receives
- Retire use cases that do not deliver value, and add new ones as needs change
- Revisit the governance rules when tools or regulations change

**Output:** a regular review, with actions and owners.

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Sponsor | Sets direction, removes obstacles and owns the decision to proceed |
| Adoption lead | Runs the programme day to day and owns the plan |
| Security and data protection | Advise on risk and approve tools and uses of data |
| Champions | Support colleagues, share examples and pass feedback back |
| Service desk | Answers questions, resolves problems and reports recurring themes |
| Team managers | Encourage use, make time for training and judge the value |
| Users | Use the tools responsibly and check the output before relying on it |

## Common pitfalls

| Pitfall | What to do instead |
| --- | --- |
| Starting with the tool rather than the problem | Start with the tasks people want help with |
| Rolling out to everyone on day one | Pilot first, then extend in stages |
| Rules that are long, vague or hard to find | One or two pages of plain language, shared widely |
| Training once and moving on | Keep enablement going with champions and regular tips |
| Support teams finding out last | Brief the service desk before each stage |
| Measuring licences issued rather than use | Measure active use and the value it delivers |
| Treating AI output as always correct | Make checking the output part of every use case |
